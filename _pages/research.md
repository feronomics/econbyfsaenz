My research interests center on development economics, human capital, and nutrition. My work also examines remittances, productive efficiency, and income dynamics in Guatemala.

## Published research

### Evaluation of the impact of remittances: Theoretical perspectives and SARIMA projection for Guatemala

With O. Herrera, E. Ipiña, and J. Jurado. **Landívar Economics**, 2(04), 37–58 (2024).

We use a SARIMA model and data from the Bank of Guatemala to study trends and seasonal patterns in family remittances, examining their outlook and implications for the Guatemalan economy.

[Read the article](https://doi.org/10.36631/LER.2024.01.02.03)

## Accepted research

### Technical efficiency in the use of materials by Guatemala's productive sectors: A stochastic frontier analysis

With S. Castillo. **ECO Revista Académica**. Accepted, forthcoming.

We apply stochastic frontier analysis with an extended Cobb–Douglas specification to compare material-use efficiency across Guatemala's productive sectors and examine the relationship between economic activity and material extraction.

## Work in progress

### The Effect of Human Capital Loss during COVID-19 on Youth Labor Market Outcomes in Guatemala

With R. Mérida. Submitted to **Landívar Economics**, under revision.

We use a difference-in-differences approach to examine labor market outcomes for the cohort that completed its final year of high school during the pandemic, focusing on employment and earnings.

### Doomed to Poverty? Empirical Evidence from Guatemala's Income Dynamics

With A. Milian and J. Velásquez. Work in progress.

Using household survey data and a pseudo-panel approach, this project investigates income dynamics and potential poverty traps in Guatemala, including how income thresholds relate to labor market conditions and minimum wages.

## Research experience

**J-PAL LAC:** I support a study led by Karen Macours on constraints to scaling climate-resilient biofortified maize among smallholder farmers in Guatemala. My contributions include survey design, enumerator training, high-frequency data checks, qualitative interviews, analysis updates, and coordination of maize sample processing and blinded laboratory procedures. I have also supported early childhood development evaluation design and fieldwork for a women's empowerment evaluation.

**ASIES Guatemala:** My work covered policy continuity, remittances and rural development, circular economy, and financial inclusion. Contributions included literature reviews, survey and interview instruments, administrative data harmonization, empirical design, and research reports.
